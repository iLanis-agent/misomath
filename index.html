Miso math

Every miso recipe says "ferment 6 months" and stops there. Whether your crock turns sweet-white in eight weeks or spoils in four is salt arithmetic - and nobody shows the working.

Run the salt before the beans cool

The problem it solves

Salt percentage is the line between aged miso and a spoiled crock, but recipes bury it in volume measures and vibes: "a cup of salt" without saying per what. The safe range is narrow - about 5% for fast sweet styles, 8% for everyday shinshu, 12% for long red - and it is measured against the whole batch weight, beans and koji together.

Miso math runs the multiplication: cooked-bean weight from your dry beans, koji by the style's ratio, salt by the style's percentage, and the honest ferment window. Then a salt check for the batch you already packed, and a window check for the crock already aging in the closet.

Salt percentages are labeled published norms, not a safety verdict. Off smells or colored mold mean discard the batch.
